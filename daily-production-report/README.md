# Daily production report

When a shoot day wraps, the production coordinator gets a draft daily report from the call sheet, timecards and department reports: hours totaled as written, schedule changes noted, and every missing or overlapping entry listed as a query. The production manager checks it.

*Workflow template · Media and entertainment · Project and delivery · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/daily-production-report/)*

## The outcome

The report is on the production manager's desk the evening the day wraps, in the house format, instead of being pieced together from a dozen emails the next morning. It says what was planned, what the departments report got done, what changed in the schedule, and how many hours the day's records add up to, by department and in total.

The usual gaps are on the page before anyone relies on the numbers: a person on the call sheet with no timecard, a card with no wrap time, one person logged on two sets at once, hours that do not match the times beside them, someone working who was never on the call sheet. Each one is a plain question with the facts beside it and a name to ask. Nothing is filled in, corrected or guessed.

The hours are the hours as written on the cards. The report applies no pay rule, no overtime, no meal-penalty and no guild computation, and it submits nothing to payroll. Those stay with the people responsible for them.

## Who starts the work, and when

The production coordinator starts it once the day has wrapped and the department records are arriving, usually that evening or early the next morning. It can be run again when a late card or a corrected entry comes in.

The production manager checks the report and decides what goes to the production office, the department heads and the cost report.

## What they supply

- **The call sheet for the day:** the crew list with call times, the planned wrap, the location and the scenes or work planned.
- **The timecard export for the day,** one row per entry, with the default headers named in the template.
- **The department reports,** as written by each department head.
- **The production notes** for the day, including any schedule change called during it.
- **Your report template,** if you have one. Without it the template's own layout is used.

No connection to a payroll system or accounting package is needed, and none is asked for.

## What comes out

One report in a fixed order: the day at a glance, the work planned and reported, schedule changes, hours by department with a day total, and numbered queries in a set order. Where a section has nothing to report, it says so rather than disappearing.

It closes with a plain line saying the hours are totaled as written, no pay rule has been applied and nothing has been sent or submitted.

## What has to change for your company

Put your own report layout in place of the default, and keep every slot the template names. Set how your timecard export is headed. Decide the tolerance for hours that differ from the times beside them, and who answers a query for each department. Say who may receive the report and when. If your production runs overnight or across time zones, say how a day is cut. Pay rules, overtime, meal penalties and guild matters stay with the specialist who owns them; the report never touches them.
