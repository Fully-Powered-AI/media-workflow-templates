# Story brief from the reporting file

Before a story is commissioned, the editor gets one brief from the reporting file: what the file confirms, what earlier coverage exists, what is still unanswered and a proposed structure, with every conflicting or unverified claim listed as a reporting gap.

*Workflow template · Media and entertainment · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/story-brief-from-reporting-file/)*

## The outcome

The editor decides on a pitch with the whole file in view instead of the reporter's summary of it. Each claim the story would rest on appears once, with who said it, where it was recorded and how well the file supports it. The pitch's central assertions are each given a standing, so the editor sees at a glance which ones the file carries and which it does not.

Where two accounts disagree, where a claim comes from one person only or from someone anonymous, or where a document was requested and has not arrived, the brief lists it as a reporting gap in plain words. Earlier coverage is read from the index the desk supplies, so nobody commissions a story the publication has already run or one that contradicts what it ran last month.

## Who starts the work, and when

The editor, or the desk coordinator on the editor's behalf, starts it when a pitch is on the table and a decision to commission or to draft is due. The reporter supplies the file and answers the questions the brief raises. The AI assistant prepares the brief. It verifies no fact, contacts no source, searches nothing and gives no view on legal exposure. The editor checks the evidence, chooses the angle and approves commissioning.

## What they supply

- **The pitch,** in the editor's or reporter's words, with the decision it feeds and the date it is due.
- **The publication profile:** audience, format and length, and the rule the publication applies before it states something as fact.
- **The reporting file:** the reporter's notes, one statement per row, with source, source type and how it was obtained.
- **The source documents** the notes lean on, as text or pages.
- **The prior coverage index:** the publication's own earlier stories on the topic, and any others the desk has listed, each with a short summary.

## What comes out

One brief in a fixed order: the pitch's central assertions and where each stands; the sources; every claim with its standing; the reporting gaps; prior coverage; unanswered questions; claims that name people or organisations; a proposed structure showing which sections can stand today and which wait on a gap; and the questions that remain about the brief itself. Empty sections say so. Nothing is sent and no record is changed.

## What has to change for your company

Set the rule your publication applies before something is stated as fact, and the way it treats anonymous and background sources. Agree your own labels for source types and keep the reporting file in the column order the template expects. Say how many central assertions a pitch may carry, how long a brief may run and who receives it. Decide what a prior coverage index must hold and who keeps it current. Add the places your standards process needs a name beside a claim, and who is told when one appears.
