# Story brief from the reporting file: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The publication, the town, the council, the people, the documents and every figure are invented, and none describes a real place or event. The publication is a local weekly, the Tallowby Courier. Its section editor has a pitch on a new recycling depot and must decide by Friday 13 November 2026 whether to commission a 1,200-word feature. The news desk coordinator runs the workflow template on Tuesday 10 November 2026.

First come the request and the inputs, exactly as the coordinator pasted them. Then the brief that came back.

## What the coordinator supplied

### The request

```
Today is Tue 10 Nov 2026.
For: section editor, local news desk, the Tallowby Courier.
Prepared by: news desk coordinator.
Decision: commission a 1,200-word feature, due Fri 13 Nov 2026.
Pitch: "Tallowby's new recycling depot will open a year late and cost about
double its budget, and residents are paying for the overrun through the waste
levy."
Central assertions:
A1 The depot opens about a year late.
A2 The cost has about doubled.
A3 Residents are paying for the overrun through the household waste levy.
```

### The publication profile

```
Audience: residents of Tallowby and the surrounding villages.
Format: local feature, 1,200 words.
Rule before a fact is stated in the paper's own voice: one primary document,
or two independent sources. An anonymous source needs the editor's approval
and one corroboration. A claim about a named person or organisation needs
their reply recorded, or an attempt to reach them logged.
```

## The brief that came back

```
STORY BRIEF: Tallowby recycling depot, delay and cost

For: Section editor, local news desk, for the decision on commissioning a 1,200-word feature, by Fri 13 Nov 2026. Prepared by: news desk coordinator, Tue 10 Nov 2026.
Pitch: "Tallowby's new recycling depot will open a year late and cost about double its budget, and residents are paying for the overrun through the waste levy."
Rule used: the publication's rule as supplied. One primary document, or two independent sources. An anonymous source needs the editor's approval and one corroboration. A claim about a named person or organisation needs their reply recorded, or an attempt to reach them logged.
The file: 8 notes from 6 sources, read as 11 claims. Prior coverage: 4 index entries, 3 used.
Standing of the claims: Confirmed in the file 2. Single-source 4. Unverified 1. Conflicting 4.

THE PITCH'S CENTRAL ASSERTIONS
A1. The depot opens about a year late: not confirmed. Rests on C2 (Confirmed in the file) and C3 (Single-source). The dates are seasons, so there is no "Worked out" line.
A2. The cost has about doubled: conflicting. Rests on C1 (Confirmed in the file), C4 and C6 (Conflicting). Worked out from C1, C4: 7.9 divided by 4.2 is 1.9 times. Worked out from C1, C6: 9 divided by 4.2 is 2.1 times.
A3. Residents are paying for the overrun through the household waste levy: conflicting. Rests on C7 and C9 (Conflicting) and C8 (Single-source). No claim in the file ties the levy to the overrun, as distinct from the depot.

SOURCES
S1. Council budget paper, capital programme (D1 p.14): primary document; public council papers.
S2. Officer A, council: named on record; phone interviews, 3 Nov and 6 Nov.
S3. Source B, contractor employee: anonymous; phone call, 4 Nov.
S4. Councillor C: named on record; email, 5 Nov.
S5. Council finance report, September 2026 (D2 p.3): primary document; public council papers.
S6. Chair E, residents' group: named on record; interview, 6 Nov.

CLAIMS
C1. "Depot approved at a budget of £4.2m". S1, N1, D1 p.14. Confirmed in the file.
C2. "with completion programmed for autumn 2026". S1, N1, D1 p.14. Confirmed in the file.
C3. "The depot is now expected to open in autumn 2027." S2, N2. Single-source.
C4. "The current cost forecast is £7.9m." S2, N2. Conflicting. Conflicts with C6.
C5. "Work on the site stopped for six weeks in the summer after the ground tests failed." S3, N3. Unverified.
C6. "The cost is nearer £9m." S3, N3. Conflicting. Conflicts with C4.
C7. "The household waste levy rose from £18 to £27 a year" S4, N4. Conflicting. Conflicts with C9.
C8. "to cover the depot." S4, N4. Single-source.
C9. "Household waste levy for 2026-27: £22 per household" S5, N5, D2 p.3. Conflicting. Conflicts with C7.
C10. "The council never told residents the depot was running late." S6, N6. Single-source.
C11. "The ground-test problem was not the contractor's fault." S2, N7. Single-source.
Not a claim: N8, the reporter's note of a records request sent 7 Nov with nothing received. It is read under G3.

REPORTING GAPS: 8
G1. Conflict: C4, C6. The file gives £7.9m (Officer A) and nearer £9m (Source B) for the cost. Source B is anonymous and the file records neither the editor's approval nor a corroboration for the £9m. Which forecast is current, how is each made up, does either include the six-week stoppage, and has the editor approved the use of Source B?
G2. Conflict: C7, C9. The file gives £27 (Councillor C, no year stated) and £22 for 2026-27 (the council finance report). Which year does Councillor C's £27 refer to, and does a document in the file show the levy for that year?
G3. Primary source unavailable: C3. The reporter's note records a request, sent 7 Nov, for the council's revised programme, and nothing received. Has the council replied, and does the programme give the new opening date?
G4. Unverified: C5. Source B is anonymous and the file records neither the editor's approval nor a corroboration. Has the editor approved the use of Source B, and is there a second source or a document for the six-week stoppage?
G5. Single-source: C3. Only Officer A, who is not a primary document, gives the autumn 2027 opening. What other source or document can the reporter point to for the new opening date?
G6. Single-source: C8. Only Councillor C says the levy rose to cover the depot. What document or second source shows what the levy rise was for?
G7. Single-source: C10. Only Chair E says residents were not told. What has the council said in reply to Chair E's statement, and is an attempt to reach it logged?
G8. Single-source: C11. Only Officer A says the ground-test problem was not the contractor's fault. What is the contractor's account of the ground-test problem?

PRIOR COVERAGE (from the index supplied only)
P1. "Council approves £4.2m depot", Tallowby Courier, 14 Mar 2025: agrees with C1 (approval at £4.2m); touches C2 (the summary gives opening in autumn 2026, where C2 gives completion programmed for autumn 2026).
P2. "Depot work paused, council says brief", Tallowby Courier, 9 Jun 2026: touches the same ground as C5 without settling it. It reports a council statement that work was paused for a short survey, with no dates, so it neither supports nor contradicts the six weeks, and it gives a survey where C5 gives failed ground tests. It also touches C10 without settling it.
P3. "Levy to rise next year, council papers show", Tallowby Courier, 21 Sep 2026: agrees with C9 (£22 this year). It adds a date, a rise proposed for 2027-28, but the proposed amount is not in the summary, so it cannot be compared with C7's £27.
Not used: P4, Pellwick Gazette, 2 Oct 2025: compares recycling rates across three districts and does not bear on the depot.

UNANSWERED QUESTIONS: 6
Q1. When: when the depot will open. No confirmed claim answers; C2 gives only the programmed date. Officer A, council, has given a date (C3).
Q2. How much: what the depot now costs and the size of the overrun. No confirmed claim answers; C4 and C6 conflict. Officer A, council, has given a forecast (C4). The file names no on-record contact at the contractor.
Q3. Why: why the work was late and the cost rose. No confirmed claim answers. Officer A, council, has spoken to the ground-test problem (C11).
Q4. Who is affected: what residents pay and whether the levy rise relates to the overrun. No confirmed claim answers; C7 and C9 conflict and no claim mentions the overrun. Councillor C has spoken to the levy (C7, C8).
Q5. Who is responsible: who is responsible for the delay and the overrun. No confirmed claim answers. Officer A, council, has spoken to the contractor's part (C11).
Q6. What each party says: the contractor's account, and the council's reply to C10. No claim records either.

CLAIMS ABOUT NAMED PEOPLE OR ORGANISATIONS
C10: the council is said to have failed to tell residents the depot was running late. The file records no reply from the council and no logged attempt to reach it. Officer A was interviewed on 6 Nov, but no reply to C10 is recorded.
C11: Officer A is quoted as saying the ground-test problem was not the contractor's fault; the contractor, identified by role, is the subject. The file records no reply from the contractor and no logged attempt to reach it.
Whether any of these goes to the publication's standards process is the section editor's decision.

PROPOSED STRUCTURE (for the pitch as stated; 1,200-word local feature)
1. What was approved: the budget and the programmed opening. Rests on C1, C2, P1. Can stand on confirmed claims today.
2. The delay: the new expected opening and the stoppage. Rests on C2, C3, C5, P2. Waits on G3, G4, G5.
3. The cost: the forecast against the budget. Rests on C1, C4, C6. Waits on G1.
4. The levy: what residents pay and what for. Rests on C7, C8, C9, P3. Waits on G2, G6.
5. Why it happened and who is responsible: the ground tests and the contractor. Rests on C5, C11, P2. Waits on G4, G8.
6. What residents were told: the council's communication to residents, as Chair E describes it. Rests on C10, P2. Waits on G7.

TO SETTLE: 7
1. C7 gives no year for the rise from £18 to £27. I compared it with C9 as if both state the current levy, and marked them Conflicting. If C7 refers to another year, the conflict falls away. Which year does it mean?
2. A3 says residents pay for the overrun. No claim says the levy rise is for the overrun; C8 says "to cover the depot". Is A3 meant to cover the original cost or the overrun?
3. The rule used needs the editor's approval and one corroboration for an anonymous source. The file records neither for Source B (C5, C6).
4. C2 and C3 give seasons, not dates, so I made no "Worked out" line for A1. Is autumn 2026 to autumn 2027 what "about a year" means?
5. N8 does not say which claims the revised programme would settle. I tied it to C3 only (G3); it may also bear on C4 and C6. Which claims was it meant to settle?
6. C7 and C8 say the levy rose and why, but not who raised it, so I did not list them as claims about a party's conduct. The file implies the council. Should they be listed?
7. The rule used needs a reply recorded or an attempt logged for a claim about a named person or organisation. The file shows neither for C10 or C11.

Nothing here has been verified, and nobody has been contacted. The section editor checks the evidence, chooses the angle and decides on commissioning.
```
