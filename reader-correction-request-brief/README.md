# Reader correction request brief

When a reader disputes a fact or reports a missing credit, the editor gets one internal note: the request, the published passage, the evidence supplied and the questions only an editor can answer. A draft acknowledgement waits for the audience lead's approval.

*Workflow template · Media and entertainment · Customer support and service · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/reader-correction-request-brief/)*

## The outcome

A reader writes in to say a figure is wrong or a credit is missing. Instead of a forwarded email with a line of context, the responsible editor receives a single page. It splits the message into its separate claims, shows the exact words that were published for each one, lists what the reader supplied, and sets beside them the reporting notes the editor has cleared for review. Every gap is written as a question to the editor.

The editor then decides what happens: correct, investigate or leave as published. The workflow template makes none of that decision. It admits no error, promises no correction, reveals nothing from a protected source and offers no view on any legal question. The reader gets a courteous acknowledgement within the time your correction policy sets (if it sets one), once the audience lead has approved it.

## Who starts the work, and when

An editor's assistant or a member of the audience team starts it the day a reader's message arrives that disputes a fact, questions a quotation or reports a missing or wrong credit. It runs once per message and once per published piece. The responsible editor reads the note. The audience lead approves the acknowledgement. The assistant or audience team member sends it only after that approval.

## What they supply

- The reader's message, complete and unedited, with the date it arrived.
- The published version of the piece, with its date, byline and any later changes.
- Anything the reader attached or linked, pasted in as text or described by name.
- The reporting notes the editor is willing to show, each marked as cleared or withheld.
- The publication's correction policy and its approved wording for acknowledgements.
- Who the responsible editor and the audience lead are, and the acknowledgement time the policy sets.

## What comes out

An internal note in a fixed order. It numbers each point the reader raised, quotes the published words that point refers to, lists the reader's evidence and the cleared notes, flags anything that needs another role, and ends with numbered questions for the editor. Below it sits a draft acknowledgement of up to about 120 words that confirms receipt, names what the reader raised without agreeing or disagreeing, and says the editor will review it. Sections with nothing to report say so.

## What has to change for your company

Put your correction policy in writing, including who decides, how quickly a reader is acknowledged and what the acknowledgement may say. Name the responsible editor for each kind of piece and the audience lead who approves replies. Decide which reporting notes an assistant may see and how a note is marked as withheld. Set your closed days for counting business days. Decide who handles a request that mentions a lawyer, a regulator, a privacy claim or a request to remove a piece. Your organization keeps every decision on what is corrected and what is said in its name. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the text you supply, and it needs no connection to your publishing system.
