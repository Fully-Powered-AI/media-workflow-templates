# Shoot location logistics pack

When a location joins the shooting schedule, the location manager gets one brief for it: how people and vehicles get in, where they park, what the place offers, who to call and what each department asked for, with every unconfirmed item listed against the production checklist.

*Workflow template · Media and entertainment · Project and delivery · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/shoot-location-logistics-pack/)*

## The outcome

Everyone who needs the location reads the same brief. The location manager sees, in a fixed order, how the gate hours compare with each day's call and wrap, how many vehicles were asked for against the spaces noted, what power and facilities the place has against what the departments requested, and which contacts are named and which are still blank.

Nothing is treated as ready because someone said it would be fine. An item counts as confirmed only when the supplied log names who confirmed it and when. Every other item is listed, numbered, with what is needed, who owns it and the date the requesting department gave. The location manager and the production manager verify the arrangements and decide what to chase.

## Who starts the work, and when

The location manager or the production coordinator starts it when a location is added to the shooting schedule, and again when the schedule, a department request or a confirmation changes. The location manager reviews it first; the production manager reads the escalation section before the brief goes to department heads.

## What they supply

- The shoot brief: location, shoot days with call and wrap times, how many people, who verifies.
- The approved location notes from the recce.
- The production's own location checklist.
- The department requests, one row each, with who asked and by when.
- A contacts list and a log of what has been confirmed, by whom and on what date.
- The status of the location agreement, permits, insurance and safety review, as the production office states it.

## What comes out

One brief in a fixed order: a readiness count, access, parking, equipment and facilities, contacts, department requests, items for escalation, the checklist matched line by line, the numbered list of everything unconfirmed, and questions for the two managers. A section with nothing in it says "None."

The brief reads no location agreement for its terms. It decides nothing about permits, safety or insurance and books nothing. Those stay with the people and specialists responsible, and the brief shows where each stands.

## What has to change for your company

Replace the default checklist sections with the ones your production uses, and keep your own column headers or map them once. Decide which words on a department request send it to a specialist reviewer; the defaults cover generators, work at height, rigging, fire or flame effects, drones, road closures, water, animals, children and stunts. Decide who verifies each section, how far ahead a brief is due, and which statuses your office uses for the agreement, permits, insurance and safety review.
