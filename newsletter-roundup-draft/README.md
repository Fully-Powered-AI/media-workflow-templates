# Newsletter roundup draft

The newsletter editor hands over the stories picked for this send and gets a draft digest grouped by beat, with a short summary, source line and link for each approved story. Duplicates, corrected stories and incomplete sources are flagged for the editor, who approves any send.

*Workflow template · Media and entertainment · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/newsletter-roundup-draft/)*

## The outcome

The editor starts the roundup from a finished draft instead of a blank page. Each approved story has a summary of a line or two, written in the publication's voice, with its byline, date and link beside it, and the stories sit under their beats in the order the newsletter uses.

The draft is honest about its own gaps. Two stories on the same decision are marked as possible duplicates. A story that has since been corrected stays out until the editor says what the roundup should do. A story whose text is cut short or whose link is missing is listed, not summarized from a guess. The summaries say what the stories say and nothing more.

## Who starts the work, and when

The newsletter editor, or an audience producer working for one, starts it when the week's or day's coverage has been selected and the stories are ready to be summarized. The editor reads the draft and the check, settles each question, and decides what goes out. Someone on the team sends it from the publication's own email tool.

## What they supply

- **The roundup settings:** the newsletter's name, the send date, who approves it and who sends it.
- **The story index,** one row per selected story: an identifier, the headline, the beat, the byline, the publication date, the link, its status (approved, corrected, held or withdrawn) and any note about reuse. If the columns are named differently, the assistant stops and asks.
- **The text of each story,** as published, labelled with its identifier.
- **The publication's voice:** a short note or two or three sample items, and the newsletter's format and beat order.
- **The editor's priority notes:** the lead story, anything to rank first and anything to leave out.

Nothing is connected to your publishing system or email tool. A person pastes everything in.

## What comes out

1. **A roundup check** for the editor: the questions to settle before the send, and every story left out of the draft with the reason and what would bring it in.
2. **A draft digest:** an optional lead story, then the beats in order, each story with its headline, summary, identifier, byline, date and link. It is marked not ready while anything is unsettled.

The digest adds no claim beyond the stories. The assistant sends nothing and schedules nothing.

## What has to change for your company

Name the editor who approves each send and the person who sends it. Fix the beat order and the shape of an item. Put two or three sample items in the publication's voice into the instructions so the summaries sound like you. Set the date window, the length limit for a summary, the item limit and the minimum story text that counts as complete. Decide what your story index calls each status and how your team records a reuse note, for example for wire copy, freelance work or a licensed photo. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini; decide which stories and notes the assistant may be given.
